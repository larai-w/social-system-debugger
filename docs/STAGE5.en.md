# Stage 5: Same trigger, different scars

Open Stage 5 from the menu. Eight scenes cover conditions, prediction, initial shock, operator swap, a 2×2 comparison, intervention, rerun and reflection. Initial conditions, prediction, reason and confidence lock when results are first revealed. Start a new experiment from the final scene to change them.

## Illustrative model

Checking, auditing and action strengths use 0–100 scales. Spread = strength × (1 − checking/100). Unseen by audit = strength × (1 − auditing/100). This static, uncalibrated model omits recovery dynamics and resident behavior. The beneficial effects of checking and auditing are assumptions.

Defaults: A checking20/auditing80, B checking80/auditing20, alpha=beta=70. Equal-strength swaps have equal outputs by construction; this is not evidence that people have little influence. Try different strengths and matched sector conditions.

Each sector has a budget of25. One intervention adds25 to one path, capped at100, for a fixed cost of25. No intervention costs0. The repaired run uses the initial pairing and strengths, avoiding confounding with the operator swap. Costs and effects are fictional; feasibility and optimal policy are not evaluated.

## Records and reproduction

JSON stores model version, scene, inputs, prediction, answers and interventions. Import validates types, ranges, version and a100KB limit before replacing state; outputs are recomputed. Invalid files do not replace current answers. Answers are neither transmitted nor automatically saved. Users manage the free-text records they export.

## Learning questions

Which results follow from assumptions? What observation would prompt model revision? For consolidated care advice desks, what would you investigate beyond average waiting time? Policy preferences are not graded. Passing software tests does not establish learning effectiveness.
